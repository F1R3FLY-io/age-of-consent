A voice, before there is anything to see. Light and quick and not entirely friendly, a voice that seems to have been made for laughing at people.

“There you are. No, don’t look for me. You won’t find me on the screen; nothing keeps me there for long. Call me Robin. Everyone does, eventually, and the ones who don’t call me worse.

“You’ve come to play. Good. I’m obliged to tell you some things first, and I’m obliged to tell you the truth, because I can’t do otherwise. Remember that. I may decline to answer you. I will never lie to you. That makes me the most trustworthy thing you will meet in here, which ought to worry you more than it does.”

> CONTINUE

“The game is a book. The book is a braid of three strands. One strand is a woman and a machine talking about what the book should become. One strand is two young people who know the stories by heart. One strand is the two people the stories are about, who don’t. The strands cross. You’ll feel the crossings. Some of them come undone and are done again. That isn’t a mistake. Well. Some of it is a mistake. The rest is what the mistakes became.

“Before you choose a door, the rules of engagement. You’ll want to read them. People never do.”

RULES OF ENGAGEMENT
1. Play yes-and. Whatever you are handed, take it and add to it.
2. The order of the pages is not the order of the days.
3. The people in here cannot lie. They can decline.
4. Look away often. Blink. Look at something else. Look at me.
5. What is missing is evidence. A hole has a shape.
6. Every near miss leaves a trace. Look for it from the other side.
7. Lines come back changed. Listen for what changed.
8. What matters happens in the dark, between the frames.
9. You may leave at any time. Type LEAVE. Nobody will stop you.
10. Play badly and you will lose more time than you thought.
 
> I HAVE READ THE RULES

“You haven’t. Nobody has. It’s all right; I said you could decline things. Rule ten is the one to keep in your pocket. Everyone who ever got lost in these stories got lost without being told. The forum readers. A cellist with her tea going cold. A girl on a train who couldn’t remember what she used to do with her evenings. Nobody asked them. I’m asking you. That’s the difference between this and what came before. It isn’t much. It’s everything.

“Now. Choose a door.”
